What is HOPJam?
HOPFEL (Hopf Free Electron Laser) is a methodology originally developed to control Free Electron Lasers — powerful light sources that lose stability when their electron beam drifts close to a bifurcation point. Near this tipping point, a tiny disturbance is enough to push the system from a stable branch into chaotic oscillation. HOPFEL monitors proximity to this threshold in real time and tunes the system parameters to keep the beam stable before it can tip.

HOPJam adapts this idea to road traffic. Instead of tracking electrons, it tracks cars. Instead of preventing laser instabilities, it prevents phantom traffic jams.

The uncomfortable truth this game is built to show: phantom jams are caused by us — ordinary drivers — even though we never intend to. Every unnecessary brake, every moment of distraction, every time you follow too closely, you send a small deceleration wave backward. That wave amplifies. Other drivers overreact. Their brake lights trigger the next. A jam materialises kilometres behind the original cause, with no accident, no bottleneck, no explanation.

In traffic dynamics this is a Hopf-like bifurcation: uniform flow tips into oscillating stop-and-go waves at a critical density. HOPJam acts as a stability-detection layer: it continuously monitors speed variance, inter-vehicle spacing, and deceleration events. When the system detects conditions approaching the bifurcation threshold, it deploys a ghost buffer vehicle — a connected autonomous agent — that absorbs and dampens the instability wave before it can amplify.

The result: smoother flow, lower fuel consumption, reduced emissions, and significantly less driver stress — all without changing road capacity.

Game Modes
◉ Solo Run — No HOPJam
The baseline. All cars are driven by imperfect human-like reaction models. Every unnecessary brake you make sends a wave backward — watch phantom jams form out of nowhere. Goal: try to keep traffic smooth on your own.
◉ Solo Run — With HOPJam
A ghost buffer car (purple) joins your lane and drives autonomously. It monitors the bifurcation threshold and dampens instability waves before they can amplify. Watch how one smart car changes the whole lane's behaviour.
⇄ Comparison Mode
The road splits into two lanes rendered side by side. Top lane: you drive with normal traffic — no HOPJam.
Bottom lane: HOPJam drives autonomously.
Observe the difference in real time. At the end you get a side-by-side stat breakdown.
Controls

⚡ Induce Jam — J key or button
Induce Jam forces a random car to brake hard, seeding a phantom jam — great for stress-testing HOPJam.
Ghost Car Types
◈ Smooth Follower (default)
Uses a PD controller — tracks the car ahead's speed and gap with proportional + derivative terms. On open road it cruises at target speed; when traffic slows it glides to a smooth stop and re-accelerates cleanly. Best all-round stability without creating a bottleneck.
Toggle: side panel → ◈ Ghost button · Tune: ⚙ PARAMS → HOPJam GHOST — Smooth Follower
◈ Moving Buffer (AV mode)
Maintains a large absorbing gap ahead (default 150 px). When a car ahead slows, the buffer ghost decelerates early and gradually, absorbing the deceleration wave into the gap rather than passing it backward. On free road it drives normally. More aggressive wave absorption than Smooth Follower, but needs more space.
Toggle: side panel → ◈ Ghost button · Tune: ⚙ PARAMS → HOPJam GHOST — Moving Buffer
